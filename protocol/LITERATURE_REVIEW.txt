Literature and overlap audit, 11 October 2026

Bounded contribution: an executable audit of multilingual input-token admission
across reservation estimates and settled true-token ledgers, with controlled
translation demand, settlement-delay sensitivity, exact parity benchmarks,
and capacity-preserving separate-data entitlement calibration.

Closest work inspected
Petrov, La Malfa, Torr, Bibi (NeurIPS 2023), Language Model Tokenizers Introduce
Unfairness Between Languages. Establishes unequal multilingual token lengths,
price/latency/context effects. We do not claim this discovery. Searched full
paper text for rate limit and quota: no occurrences. Our operational distinction
is reservation error plus delayed reconciliation and joint feasibility/access.
https://papers.nips.cc/paper_files/paper/2023/hash/74bb24dca8334adce292883b4b651eda-Abstract-Conference.html

Ahia et al. (EMNLP 2023), Do All Languages Cost the Same? Tokenization in the Era
of Commercial Language Models. Commercial tokenization cost predecessor.
https://aclanthology.org/2023.emnlp-main.614/

Sheng et al. (OSDI 2024), Fairness in Serving Large Language Models. Token-cost
fair scheduler, VTC. We do not invent fair LLM serving or compare a quota test
to a GPU scheduler. The estimands and execution layers differ.
https://www.usenix.org/conference/osdi24/presentation/sheng

Khan et al. (2024 arXiv preprint), Ensuring Fair LLM Serving Amid Diverse
Applications. Application-aware throttling and weighted scheduling. Important
precedent for workload normalization; our calibrated weights are not a new
scheduling primitive. Full paper inspected, including fairness/application
sections; no tokenizer discussion found by text search.
https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.15997

Rust et al. (ACL 2021), How Good is Your Tokenizer? On the Monolingual Performance
of Multilingual Language Models. Tokenizer quality predecessor.
https://aclanthology.org/2021.acl-long.243/

Joshi et al. (ACL 2020), The State and Fate of Linguistic Diversity and Inclusion
in the NLP World. Resource/representation context.
https://aclanthology.org/2020.acl-main.560/

Blodgett et al. (ACL 2020), Language (Technology) is Power: A Critical Survey of
Bias in NLP. Grounds restricted claims and identification of harm mechanisms.
https://aclanthology.org/2020.acl-main.485/

Selbst et al. (FAT* 2019), Fairness and Abstraction in Sociotechnical Systems.
Grounds distinction between resource measurement and institutional allocation.
https://doi.org/10.1145/3287560.3287598

Local prior-work overlap
Reviewed the research-folder inventory and extracted text/abstracts for all
available PDFs. Existing themes: stochastic tool cache coupling; KL retry
reliability; delegation inference; personal memory; prefix-cache retention;
financial exposure routing; deposit control; effect-scoped compensation;
capped/collective/private agent contracts; noisy-feedback bandits; remedy tool
exposure; safe observation scheduling; shared-label evaluation; coherent
counterfactual replay. The present paper concerns admission quota measurement
and multilingual entitlements, distinct in estimand, data, code, and experiments.
Prefix-cache work shares a broad infrastructure setting but not its optimization
problem. Remedy-access work shares an access concern but tests tool exposure,
not token reservation or translated content. No old paper is resubmitted here.

Limits: this is a targeted search, not an exhaustive systematic review or patent
clearance. Absence of a returned result is not proof of global novelty. The
paper's contribution is expressly restricted and avoids a claim of inventing
weighted allocation, tokenization inequity, or accounting conservation.
